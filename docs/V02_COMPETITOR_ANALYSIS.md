### Truth Firewall baseline and actionable gaps

- [Canonical activation digest, readiness, stale-state and mandatory aggregation](https://github.com/aldi949/truth-firewall/blob/cadbcc676887e48a2520c02a1582bdb700d1ada4/src/truth_firewall/completion.py#L35); [public pilot schema](https://github.com/aldi949/truth-firewall/blob/cadbcc676887e48a2520c02a1582bdb700d1ada4/src/truth_firewall/pilot.py#L18); [retry terminal semantics](https://github.com/aldi949/truth-firewall/blob/cadbcc676887e48a2520c02a1582bdb700d1ada4/src/truth_firewall/lifecycle.py#L129); [bounded snapshot exclusions/limits](https://github.com/aldi949/truth-firewall/blob/cadbcc676887e48a2520c02a1582bdb700d1ada4/src/truth_firewall/evidence/provenance.py#L23).
- Priority gap: the public completion/pilot contract does not expose required test/command execution or test-integrity baseline guards. A passing existence check says nothing about modification or behavior; passing finite examples says nothing about omitted obligations.
- Priority safeguard to retain: separate worker DONE from authorization, fail closed on UNKNOWN/stale observations, and preserve explicit review invalidation. Do not replace these with a generic green shell exit.
- Scope caveat: richer `coverage_authority.py`/`contract_coverage.py` modules exist, but their presence does not prove public pilot wiring or natural-language completeness. The public TaskCompletionContract observed here contains conditions and review metadata, not an exhaustive semantic obligation inventory.
- Safe lessons, not a redesign request: make evidence scope explicit; differentiate existence/change/behavior; bind command evidence to observed workspace; treat omitted checks, skipped collection and partial selections as unresolved for mandatory completion. Guard regexes/counts can catch common weakening but cannot prove arbitrary assertions retain their meaning.

No winner is asserted. All three depend on humans supplying adequate acceptance criteria; none establishes arbitrary natural-language requirement completeness or an OS-level security boundary.
